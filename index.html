THE METHOD

Follow the moles.

Coefficients connect amounts in moles. Molar masses connect moles and grams.

A BALANCED EQUATION IS YOUR RECIPE

2 Mg(s)+O2(g)→2 MgO(s)

What mass of MgO can form from 12.0 g Mg with oxygen in excess?

“Oxygen in excess” means there is more than enough oxygen to react with all the magnesium. The amount of Mg determines how much MgO can form. Start with a balanced equation: 2 mol Mg make 2 mol MgO.

1

Grams to moles

Molar mass tells you the mass of one mole. Each mole of Mg has a mass of 24.31 g, so divide your grams by 24.31 g/mol.

12.0 g Mg×1 mol Mg24.31 g Mg= 0.493624… mol Mg

Put g Mg on the bottom so it cancels the starting g Mg. Moles of Mg remain.

2

Moles to moles

The balanced equation is a recipe: 2 mol Mg make 2 mol MgO. Put the coefficient of the substance you want on top; put the one you have on the bottom.

0.493624… mol Mg×2 mol MgO2 mol Mg= 0.493624… mol MgO

The ratio happens to be 1 here. With O2 as the starting substance, it would be 2 mol MgO / 1 mol O2. Always read the coefficients.

3

Moles back to grams

Each mole of MgO has a mass of 40.31 g. Multiply by the product’s molar mass to find the mass of MgO.

0.493624… mol MgO×40.31 g MgO1 mol MgO= 19.897984… g MgO

Moles of MgO cancel. Grams of MgO remain—the unit the question asks for.

Now connect the three steps.

12.0 g Mg×1 mol Mg24.31 g Mg×2 mol MgO2 mol Mg×40.31 g MgO1 mol MgO=19.9 g MgO

The crossed-out units cancel, leaving 19.9 g MgO, the theoretical yield. The intermediate decimals above are shortened for display; keep the full calculator value between steps.

FINISH WITH THE RIGHT PRECISION

Carry extra digits. Round once.

For multiplication and division, report the answer with the fewest significant figures among the measured values you use. 12.0 g has three significant figures: 1, 2, and the final 0. The listed molar masses have at least that much precision, so report three significant figures.

Balanced coefficients are exact counts; they do not limit significant figures. The factor of 100 in percent yield is exact, too.

Calculator → reported answer

19.897984… g → 19.9 g MgO

Which zeros count?
5.00
3 significant figures. Zeros after the decimal show measured precision.
0.00450
3 significant figures. Leading zeros only locate the decimal; the final zero counts.
19.90
4 significant figures. An extra zero claims extra precision.
1.20 × 102
3 significant figures. Scientific notation makes the precision clear when a whole number ends in zeros. Enter 1.20e2 in Practice.

Significant figures are not decimal places: 12.6 and 0.126 both have three significant figures.

Why does the product weigh more?

MgO contains the original magnesium plus oxygen from the other reactant. Mass is conserved across all reactants and products. Coefficients compare moles, so applying 2/2 directly to 12.0 grams would not give the product’s mass.
